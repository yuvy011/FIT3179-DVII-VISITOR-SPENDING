Domestic travellers provide most of Australia’s tourism consumption. Beneath that national total, visitors spend differently depending on their purpose, origin and destination. Those differences shape where travel dollars land and how the visitor economy has recovered since the pandemic.

01 The spending basket

What do visitors actually buy?

Domestic tourism accounts for four in five dollars of national tourism consumption. Residents’ overnight budgets are dominated by transport, accommodation and food. Overseas visitors bring a broader mix of travel and study costs, while spending inside Australia captures only part of their full-trip budget.

Of A$211.1 billion in tourism consumption during 2024 to 25, domestic visitors accounted for A$168.8b (80.0%) and international visitors A$42.3b (20.0%).

National spending baskets contain many different kinds of trips. Travel purpose and home country help explain why one visitor’s budget looks different from another’s.

02 The visitors behind the spending

Why do visitor budgets differ?

Behind every budget is a reason for travelling. Holidaymakers and students spend differently, reflecting what brings them to Australia. Looking across origin markets reveals further differences in spending priorities and average spending per trip, while the uneven recovery in arrivals shows how visitor numbers have changed.

FIGURE 06

IVS · year ending March 2026

A holiday budget and a study budget tell different stories

Different reasons for travelling produce different spending mixes, especially for education-related travel.

How to read this chart

Wider columns mean more spending. Within a column, the height of a block is that category’s share of the purpose’s spending. Education (amber) is labelled on every column; for holiday, friends and family and business it is a very thin slice, so its label sits above the bar.

FIGURE 07

IVS · year ending March 2026

China accounts for 86% of education spending across these five markets

Education spending is concentrated among Chinese visitors, who contribute A$4.63 billion of the A$5.36 billion total across the selected markets. Indiafollows at A$494.6 million, while the remaining markets account for a comparatively small share.

How to read this chart

Longer bars mean more education expenditure. The denominator is education spending across these five markets only; this is not the education share of each market’s travel budget. All bars start at zero, and the direct labels retain small shares without rounding them up to one square.

FIGURE 08

IVS · year ending March 2026

China leads four of five categories; the UK leads transport

Across the selected markets, Chinese visitors lead total spending on food, drink and accommodation, shopping, entertainment and education, while UK visitors spend the most on transport.

How to read this chart

Follow a coloured line to see how one market’s rank changes from category to category. Higher means more total spending, not more per trip or per night. Ranks compare only these five markets, and circles are all the same size, so they show rank only, not the amount spent. Use the legend to identify each market by colour.

Visitor arrivals have recovered unevenly since the pandemic

India, the UK and New Zealand exceed their pre-pandemic arrival counts; China and the USA remain below. Choose a year to compare all five flows on one map. Visitor counts update beside each origin.

How to read this chart

Radio buttons switch between the full financial years 2018–19 and 2025–26. Wider flows mean more short-term arrivals, using one fixed scale. Labels show rounded arrival counts; tooltips show exact counts for both years and the percentage change. Five coloured flows enter Australia at separate schematic anchors to remain distinguishable. Anchors do not represent airports or intended destinations, and lines are not actual flight paths. ABS counts arrival movements, not unique people. China excludes SARs and Taiwan. These are five selected markets, not all source countries.

Education shapes China’s budget; transport dominates the UK’s

Education accounts for 33.7% of Chinese visitors’ full-trip spending, compared with 0.5% for UK visitors. Transport takes 43.6% of the UK’s budget and 12.9% of China’s. These shares reveal different spending priorities within each market.

Origin markets differ in what they buy. At destinations, spending per night reveals another dimension: how visitor dollars are distributed across Australia and within its regions.

03 The geography and the recovery

Where does the money go, and how is it changing?

Visitor dollars reach destinations unevenly. Tasmania leads the state and territory comparison for international spending per night, while Queensland reveals differences within a single state. The story then shifts from place to change: transport gains spending dollars but loses budget share, while Western Australia and the ACT receive more spending overall but less per visitor-night.

FIGURE 11

IVS · year ending March 2026

Tasmania has the highest average international spend per night

Tasmania recorded the highest average international visitor spending per night among Australian states and territories in the year ending March 2026.

How to read this chart

A$ per night is what overseas visitors spend in a state divided by the nights they stay there. It is spending, not hotel price. Colours represent six spending bands; printed dollar values distinguish states within the same band. The ACT and Tasmania are labelled beside the map.

FIGURE 12

TRA IVS · year ending March 2026 · international visitors aged 15+

Within Queensland, international spending per night ranges from A$59 to A$202

International visitors spend about A$202 per night in Tropical North Queensland, compared with A$152 on the Gold Coast, A$130 in Brisbane and A$59 in Queensland Country. These regional differences show what Queensland’s statewide average of about A$135 per night can conceal.

How to read this chart

The legend circles use the same size scale, teal colours and outlines as the map circles, showing A$50, A$100, A$150 and A$200 per visitor-night. Larger, darker circles indicate higher spending per night; circle area, rather than diameter, is proportional to the amount. The map shows six Queensland tourism regions with published estimates. Regions without circles are outside this table’s coverage, not zero spending. Hover over a circle for the region’s spending per night and reporting period.

WHAT THEY BUY · WHO THEY ARE · WHERE IT GOES

A bigger tourism bill does not mean the same thing everywhere.

First, domestic tourism drives the scale. The latest Tourism Satellite Account shows around 80% of national consumption is domestic. Second, the spending mix depends on the visitor. Survey categories, travel purposes and source countries describe different behaviours, but full-trip and in-Australia totals cannot be combined. Third, growth has multiple drivers. Compare regional spending with visitor-nights and distinguish changes in nominal dollars from changes in share.

The ABS arrival comparison shows that recovery differs by origin market: three of the five selected markets exceed their 2018–19 counts, while China and the USA remain below. This describes visitor arrivals, not a recovery in real spending or tourism profitability.
